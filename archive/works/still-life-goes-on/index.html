Overview

A kinetic installation that reflects on the quiet persistence of life through a spinning wheel and a robotic moth. The work focuses on repetition, transformation, and the act of continuing.

Concept

At the center of the piece, a mechanical spinning wheel turns in a steady rhythm. When a viewer approaches, its speed and direction shift in an unstable way before slowly returning to its original motion.

Above the wheel, a robotic moth moves with a small but constant motion, as if driven by its own inner desire. The pairing of wheel and moth connects personal memories of silk and textile labor with the present moment.

The work bridges traditional material memory and contemporary media practice, using motors, sensors, real time control, and printed parts to build a story of softness, tension, and continuation.

Process

The project combines research, 3D modeling, printing, wiring, and motion tests. The spinning wheel and support structure were modeled and refined through multiple iterations to create a motion that feels both controlled and unstable.

Motors, drivers, and sensors are used so that the work can react to the presence of a viewer and then slowly return to its original cycle.

Hardware and Software

Stepper motors, DM542T driver, Arduino Uno, ultrasonic distance sensor, 3D printed PLA components, LED lighting, custom control logic, motor behavior programming.
